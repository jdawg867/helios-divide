# HELIOS DIVIDE
## Game Vision & Master Design Brief

**Working title:** Helios Divide  
**Genre:** Hard-science-fiction first/third-person shooter RPG  
**Primary mode:** Single-player story-driven open-world / hub-based action RPG  
**Future mode:** Optional co-op after the single-player foundation is stable  
**Camera:** Seamless first-person / third-person switching  
**Setting:** The inhabited Solar System, several centuries in the future  
**Tone:** Grounded, dangerous, political, cinematic, adult, morally complex  
**Core fantasy:** Own a ship, build a crew, choose your loyalties, fight on stations and ships, survive zero gravity, take contracts, influence a divided Solar System, and decide what kind of person you become.

---

# 1. High Concept

**Helios Divide** is a large-scale science-fiction shooter RPG set across a politically fractured Solar System.

Humanity has expanded beyond Earth but has not become a single civilization. Earth, Luna, Mars, the asteroid settlements, corporate habitats, independent stations, security fleets, smugglers, mercenaries, and frontier communities all depend on one another while competing for water, fuel, technology, territory, labor, and political control.

The player creates an original character and begins as an independent spacer with a small ship, a limited reputation, and almost no political protection. What begins with ordinary paid work—cargo security, bounty contracts, rescue jobs, ship recovery, investigations, smuggling, station security, salvage, and escort work—slowly exposes a covert program capable of changing the balance of power throughout the Solar System.

The player can fight in first person or third person at any time. Combat can happen in gravity, low gravity, or complete zero gravity. Players can board ships, fight through station corridors, cross exposed asteroid surfaces, use magnetic boots, maneuver with suit thrusters, negotiate with factions, build relationships with crew members, upgrade weapons and armor, customize their ship, and shape the political outcome of the campaign.

The game should feel like a believable future rather than fantasy in space. Technology can be advanced, but it should normally have understandable physical limits, costs, dangers, and tradeoffs.

---

# 2. Original-IP Rule

Helios Divide is inspired by the **type of experience** found in grounded space-political dramas and hard-science-fiction stories, but it must remain an original intellectual property.

Do **not** copy protected names, characters, factions, storylines, dialogue, logos, costumes, ship designs, alien technology, locations created specifically for another franchise, or recognizable scene recreations from *The Expanse* or any other existing property.

The game may use broad genre concepts that are not unique to one franchise, such as:

- A colonized Solar System.
- Political tension between planets and orbital settlements.
- Belters, spacers, planetary citizens, corporations, and military fleets.
- Realistic or semi-realistic space travel.
- Artificial gravity from acceleration or rotation.
- Zero-gravity combat.
- Boarding actions.
- Magnetic boots.
- Space suits and EVA.
- Resource scarcity.
- Faction politics.
- Crew relationships.
- Hard choices with long-term consequences.

Everything specific to Helios Divide—characters, organizations, visual identity, ships, lore, technology, story, dialogue, symbols, uniforms, and terminology—should be created from scratch.

# 3. Design Pillars

Every major feature should strengthen at least one of these pillars.

## 3.1 You Live in the Solar System

The world should feel inhabited, industrial, political, and interconnected.

Stations are not just combat maps. They contain homes, markets, clinics, bars, docks, customs offices, repair yards, transit hubs, security checkpoints, industrial spaces, and faction territories.

NPCs should appear to have jobs, routines, affiliations, and opinions.

## 3.2 First-Person and Third-Person Are Equal Citizens

The player can switch between first-person and third-person during normal gameplay.

Neither mode should feel like an afterthought.

First person emphasizes:
- Precision shooting.
- Immersion.
- Tight interiors.
- Weapon handling.
- EVA tension.

Third person emphasizes:
- Character visibility.
- Situational awareness.
- Cover.
- Movement.
- Melee.
- Cinematic exploration.

## 3.3 Gravity Changes Combat

Combat should behave differently depending on the environment.

Possible states:
- Standard gravity.
- Low gravity.
- Spin gravity.
- Thrust gravity.
- Zero gravity.

In zero gravity:
- Recoil can affect player movement.
- Bodies and loose objects float.
- Direction is relative.
- Magnetic boots can anchor the player.
- Suit thrusters allow six-axis movement.
- Damaging a suit can become a survival problem.
- Doors and decompression can alter encounters.

## 3.4 Choices Have Consequences

Factions remember what the player does.

Mission outcomes can change:
- Reputation.
- Prices.
- Access to stations.
- Available contracts.
- Crew loyalty.
- Security response.
- Criminal status.
- Future dialogue.
- Story missions.
- Ending states.

The game should avoid obvious “good” and “evil” buttons. Most serious decisions should involve competing interests.

## 3.5 Your Ship Is Home

The player’s ship is more than transportation.

It should act as:
- Mobile player home.
- Mission staging area.
- Crew hub.
- Inventory location.
- Upgrade platform.
- Story location.
- Travel interface.
- Tactical asset.
- Symbol of progression.

# 4. Setting

## 4.1 Time Period

The exact year can remain flexible during pre-production, but the recommended range is approximately **2300–2400 CE**.

Humanity has established permanent settlements throughout the inner and middle Solar System.

There is no everyday faster-than-light travel.

Travel time matters.

Distance matters.

Fuel matters.

Communications are fast but not magically instantaneous across all distances.

---

# 5. Political State of the Solar System

Humanity is divided into major power blocs.

## 5.1 Terran Compact

The Terran Compact is centered on Earth and Luna.

It controls enormous population centers, financial institutions, major orbital infrastructure, diplomatic networks, and some of the oldest interplanetary military assets.

**Strengths**
- Population.
- Money.
- Diplomacy.
- Established industry.
- Large security fleet.

**Weaknesses**
- Bureaucracy.
- Political fragmentation.
- Dependence on off-world resources.
- Unequal access to opportunity.
- Aging infrastructure.

The Compact publicly presents itself as the stabilizing center of human civilization.

Its enemies describe it as an old empire unwilling to admit that the rest of humanity has grown beyond it.

## 5.2 Ares Commonwealth

Mars and its orbital settlements form the Ares Commonwealth.

Mars has spent generations transforming itself into an industrial and technological power.

**Strengths**
- Advanced engineering.
- Highly trained military.
- Shipbuilding.
- Scientific institutions.
- Strong civic identity.

**Weaknesses**
- Harsh environment.
- Dependence on life-support infrastructure.
- Political pressure between civilian and military leadership.
- Resource competition.

The Commonwealth believes humanity survives by discipline, engineering, and long-term planning.

Its critics believe Mars has built a society that can justify almost anything in the name of survival.

## 5.3 Free Orbit Coalition

The Free Orbit Coalition is not a conventional nation.

It is a loose network of asteroid habitats, industrial stations, mining settlements, independent ports, trade unions, transport crews, and autonomous local governments.

**Strengths**
- Resource access.
- Experienced spacers.
- Decentralized logistics.
- Local knowledge.
- Adaptability.

**Weaknesses**
- Internal disagreement.
- Uneven military capability.
- Economic dependence.
- Vulnerability to blockade.
- Local rivalries.

Some members want independence.

Some want better trade terms.

Some want cooperation with the inner planets.

Some want revolution.

The Coalition is intentionally politically diverse.

## 5.4 Helix Combine

The Helix Combine is a corporate industrial alliance with operations spread across the Solar System.

It is not officially a sovereign power, but in some locations it effectively behaves like one.

Helix operates:
- Shipyards.
- Mining platforms.
- Research facilities.
- Security contractors.
- Shipping networks.
- Fuel depots.
- Private habitats.

Its influence comes from controlling infrastructure everyone needs.

## 5.5 The Black Current

The Black Current is a loose criminal network rather than a unified faction.

It includes:
- Smugglers.
- Information brokers.
- Hijackers.
- Illegal salvagers.
- Contract killers.
- Pirate crews.
- Corrupt officials.
- Black-market shipyards.

Some members are violent predators.

Others see themselves as people surviving outside systems that were already corrupt.

The player may work against them, infiltrate them, cooperate with them, or become associated with them.

# 6. The Central Conflict

The Solar System is already unstable when the campaign begins.

Shipping lanes are increasingly militarized.

Several stations have experienced unexplained power failures.

Military patrols are moving outside their normal sectors.

A number of independent cargo vessels have disappeared.

Rumors spread about a classified propulsion program called **ORPHEUS**.

Officially, ORPHEUS does not exist.

In reality, several governments and private interests have been secretly competing to develop a next-generation fusion torch system capable of producing dramatically higher sustained acceleration than existing civilian drives.

The breakthrough itself is not magical.

It is dangerous, expensive, difficult to manufacture, and potentially destabilizing.

A ship equipped with a mature ORPHEUS drive could:
- Cross major distances faster.
- Intercept ships previously considered safe.
- Deliver military forces with little warning.
- Make existing defensive strategies obsolete.
- Turn ordinary drive output into a terrifying weapon.

The player gradually discovers that the technology is being hidden, stolen, sabotaged, copied, and manipulated by several competing groups.

There is no single simple villain.

The main story becomes a struggle over who should control a technology that could reshape civilization.

- Pronouns.
- Origin.
- Professional background.
- Starting skill bonus.

- Social connections.
- Administrative knowledge.
- Negotiation bonuses in Compact territory.

## Martian
Raised in the Ares Commonwealth.

Advantages:
- Technical knowledge.
- Military familiarity.
- Engineering bonuses.

## Spacer
Raised primarily aboard ships or stations.

Advantages:
- EVA proficiency.
- Zero-gravity movement.
- Ship systems familiarity.

## Frontier
Raised in an independent settlement.

Advantages:
- Survival.
- Salvage.
- Improvised equipment.
- Independent-faction reputation.

The origin changes dialogue and small opportunities but should not lock the player out of major play styles.

# 10. Core Gameplay Loop

The primary loop is:

1. Arrive at a station or settlement.
2. Explore.
3. Talk to NPCs.
4. Check faction standing.
5. Acquire contracts or story missions.
6. Gather intelligence.
7. Buy supplies.
8. Select weapons, armor, and tools.
9. Prepare crew.
10. Travel.
11. Approach the mission location.
12. Dock, EVA, land, or board.
13. Investigate, fight, negotiate, infiltrate, hack, rescue, steal, or salvage.
14. Make mission decisions.
15. Escape or secure the area.
16. Return to ship.
17. Receive payment, reputation, evidence, loot, or consequences.
18. Upgrade character or ship.
19. React to changes in the world.
20. Choose the next destination.

# 13. Combat

Combat should feel lethal but readable.

The player should not absorb unrealistic amounts of gunfire.

Survival comes from:
- Armor.
- Cover.
- Positioning.
- Movement.
- Medical treatment.
- Environmental awareness.
- Crew support.
- Equipment choices.

## Damage Types

- Ballistic.
- Fragmentation.
- Thermal.
- Electrical.
- Vacuum exposure.
- Radiation.
- Impact trauma.

## Body Effects

Optional deeper simulation:
- Limb injuries.
- Bleeding.
- Concussion.
- Suit breach.
- Reduced mobility.
- Reduced weapon stability.

The system should be deep enough to create interesting decisions without becoming tedious.

Weapons should reflect practical space combat.

## Ballistic Weapons

- SMGs.
- Carbines.
- Battle rifles.
- Precision rifles.
- Shotguns.
- Light machine guns.

## Electromagnetic Weapons
- Coil pistols.
- Coil carbines.

- Restraint launchers.

# 23. Encounter Design

Good encounters should offer multiple approaches.

Example: Recover a stolen reactor-control module.

Possible solutions:
- Assault the station.
- Sneak through maintenance tunnels.
- Bribe a dock worker.
- Hack access credentials.
- Negotiate with the thieves.
- Buy the module back.
- Frame a competing faction.
- Trigger an evacuation and steal it during the confusion.

The result should affect later events.

# 30. Main Campaign Structure

## Act I — Independent Spacer

The player acquires or inherits control of a small cutter.

Initial jobs introduce:
- Shooting.
- Dialogue.
- Travel.
- EVA.
- Reputation.
- Ship upgrades.
- Crew.

A salvage contract reveals evidence connected to ORPHEUS.

## Act II — Fault Lines

Multiple factions begin searching for the same evidence.

The player must decide who receives information.

Crew conflicts become more important.

Stations react to growing political tension.

## Act III — The Divide

An incident involving an ORPHEUS prototype pushes the major factions toward open conflict.

The player can:
- Support one power.
- Attempt to expose all participants.
- Back an independent coalition.
- Sell information.
- Destroy critical technology.
- Help create a negotiated control framework.

## Act IV — Burn Line

The final act is determined by previous alliances.

The final missions should vary significantly depending on the player’s choices.

Health should not instantly regenerate from severe injury.

Possible system:
- Minor health recovery through basic treatment.
- Serious injuries require medkits.
- Trauma effects persist until treated.
- Ship med station restores full condition.

Medical skill improves:
- Treatment speed.
- Efficiency.
- Injury stabilization.

# 41. Art Direction

Visual style:
- Functional.
- Industrial.
- Used future.
- Believable engineering.
- Strong faction identity.

Avoid:
- Excessive glowing neon everywhere.
- Fantasy-looking technology without purpose.
- Perfectly clean ships unless appropriate.
- Generic “blue hologram” interfaces for every system.

Faction design differences:

**Terran Compact:** old institutional design, standardized, layered upgrades.

**Ares Commonwealth:** precise, compact, advanced, military-industrial.

**Free Orbit Coalition:** repaired, modular, highly personalized.

**Helix Combine:** clean corporate engineering, branded infrastructure.

**Black Current:** stolen equipment, mixed hardware, improvised modifications.

# 42. Audio Direction

Important sounds:
- Hull vibration.
- Ventilation.
- Pumps.
- Magnetic boots.
- Airlocks.
- Reactor hum.
- Radio chatter.
- Distant machinery.
- Weapon impulse.
- Alarm systems.
- Suit breathing.
- Thruster bursts.

In vacuum, external sound should be treated carefully.

The player may hear transmitted or structure-borne sound through the suit rather than conventional atmosphere.

# 46. Difficulty

Difficulty should change:
- Enemy accuracy.
- Tactical aggression.
- Damage tolerance.
- Resource scarcity.
- Injury severity.

Avoid turning high difficulty into nothing but enormous enemy health bars.

Optional simulation settings can increase:
- Oxygen management.
- Injury persistence.
- Ammunition scarcity.
- Ship repair costs.

# 47. Multiplayer Direction

Do not make multiplayer a dependency for the first playable version.

Build the single-player game first.

Possible later co-op:
- 2–4 players.
- Host owns ship and campaign.
- Other players join as crew.
- Shared boarding missions.
- Shared EVA.
- Tactical ship roles.

Do not redesign the entire game around live-service monetization.

# 48. Monetization Philosophy

# 55. Milestone Definition of Done

A feature is not complete only because it visually works once.

A feature should be considered complete when:
- It works in a clean project run.
- It handles basic failure cases.
- It does not break existing systems.
- Inputs are configurable.
- Important state saves correctly.
- Basic documentation exists.

# 56. Seely.ai Master Build Brief

Use the following as the initial build specification when creating the project in an AI game-development system.

---

## MASTER BUILD PROMPT

Create a new original science-fiction game titled **Helios Divide**.

Helios Divide is a grounded hard-science-fiction first/third-person shooter RPG set in a politically divided, colonized Solar System several centuries in the future.

The game must be an original IP. Do not copy characters, faction names, storylines, dialogue, ship designs, logos, visual assets, or unique technology from The Expanse or any other existing franchise.

The player creates a customizable independent spacer and owns a small multipurpose spacecraft. The player can explore stations and ships, take contracts, build a crew, join or oppose factions, fight enemies, conduct EVA operations, board spacecraft, investigate political conspiracies, upgrade equipment, and influence the future of the Solar System.

The player must be able to switch seamlessly between first-person and third-person gameplay.

The game must support environments with:
- Standard gravity.
- Low gravity.
- Zero gravity.
- Vacuum.
- Pressurized interiors.

Core player features:
- Walk.
- Sprint.
- Crouch.
- Jump.
- Mantle.
- Interact.
- Aim.
- Fire.
- Reload.
- Switch weapons.
- First/third-person camera toggle.
- Inventory.
- Equipment.
- Health.
- Armor.
- EVA suit oxygen.
- Magnetic boots.
- Zero-G thrusters.

Combat should be tactical and relatively lethal. Enemies should use cover, flank, retreat, react to alarms, and behave differently in zero gravity.

Build a modular weapon system supporting pistols, carbines, shotguns, rifles, ammunition, reloads, recoil, optics, attachments, damage, and armor.

Build RPG systems for:
- Skills.
- Inventory.
- Equipment.
- Dialogue.
- Quest state.
- Faction reputation.
- Crime.
- Companion trust.
- Credits.
- Vendors.

Major factions:
1. Terran Compact — Earth/Luna political and economic power.
2. Ares Commonwealth — Mars-based engineering and military power.
3. Free Orbit Coalition — decentralized asteroid and station communities.
4. Helix Combine — massive corporate infrastructure alliance.
5. Black Current — loose criminal and smuggling network.

Central story:
A secret high-performance fusion propulsion program called ORPHEUS threatens to destabilize the balance of power. Several factions are trying to steal, control, destroy, expose, or regulate the technology. The player becomes involved after recovering evidence from a missing cargo vessel.

The player's starter ship is a customizable Morrow-class independent cutter.

Begin development with a vertical slice rather than the entire game.

Vertical slice:
- One station hub called Meridian Station.
- One player ship interior.
- One abandoned cargo ship.
- One EVA exterior section.
- One recruitable companion, Rhea Calder.
- Four usable weapons.
- One armor set.
- One EVA suit.
- Basic inventory.
- Dialogue.
- Quest tracking.
- Reputation.
- Enemy AI.
- Save/load.
- First-person mode.
- Third-person mode.
- Perspective switching.
- Zero-G movement.
- Magnetic boots.
- Airlocks.
- Oxygen.
- Basic environmental pressure state.

First mission: **Dead Freight**.

The player accepts a contract to investigate a cargo vessel that never reached Meridian Station. The player travels to the disabled ship, crosses a damaged exterior section by EVA, enters through an emergency airlock, restores power, discovers signs of a hijacking, encounters survivors and attackers, and recovers a data core containing evidence related to ORPHEUS.

The mission must allow at least three approaches:
- Combat.
- Stealth.
- Negotiation.

At the end, the player decides which faction receives the recovered evidence. That decision changes reputation and future quest availability.

Create systems as modular components so the game can grow into a larger Solar-System RPG.

Do not attempt to generate the entire final game immediately.

First create the project foundation and vertical-slice architecture.

- Become a mercenary.
- Become a bounty hunter.
- Become a smuggler.
- Become a pirate.
